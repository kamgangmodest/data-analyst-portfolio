# Business Insights

This project uses **synthetic retail data** created for portfolio demonstration.

## Executive Summary
- Total revenue: **$140,051**
- Gross profit: **$61,518**
- Gross margin: **43.9%**
- Units sold: **1,655**
- Highest-revenue store: **Washington DC** ($38,866)
- Highest-revenue category: **Office** ($71,874)
- Highest-revenue product: **Standing Desk** ($45,291)
- Transaction rows at or below reorder level: **114**

## What the analysis shows
1. **Store performance is uneven.** Washington DC contributes the most revenue, so leadership should compare its product mix and sales patterns with lower-performing locations.
2. **Office leads category revenue.** Inventory availability for this category should be monitored closely because stockouts could have an outsized revenue impact.
3. **Standing Desk is the top product by revenue.** Replenishment and promotional decisions should protect availability while maintaining margin.
4. **Inventory risk exists alongside sales growth.** Low-stock flags identify product/store combinations where replenishment should be prioritized.
5. **Profit should be evaluated with revenue.** Discounting can increase units sold while compressing margin, so the dashboard tracks both revenue and gross profit.

## Recommended actions
- Create reorder alerts for products at or below their reorder level.
- Review product mix in lower-performing stores and compare it with the top store.
- Protect high-revenue, high-margin SKUs from stockouts.
- Track monthly discount rate alongside gross margin to avoid unnecessary margin erosion.
- Use category and store drill-downs to separate demand issues from inventory issues.
